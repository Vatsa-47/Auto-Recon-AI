THE CHALLENGE

The Modern Automotive Forensic Problem

01

Multiple Heterogeneous Sources

Automotive incidents generate disparate evidence across EDR, CAN, ECU, GPS, and dashcam.

02

Isolated Data Silos

EDR, CAN logs, ECU fault tables, and sensor channels are routinely analyzed in complete isolation.

03

Manual Log Correlation

Investigators must align asynchronous timestamps and packet sequences by hand across days of work.

04

Cyber vs Physical Disconnect

Connecting network anomalies or CAN bus spoofing with physical actuator responses is notoriously difficult.

05

Fragmented Evidence

Critical evidence fragments across multiple proprietary OEM toolsets without a unified chain of custody.

The key challenge: How can investigators reconstruct what actually happened from heterogeneous automotive evidence?

Evidence Strength Grading
  • • Confirmed Evidence: Deterministic EDR/CAN data match.
  • • Probable Contributing Factor: Secondary environmental/chassis cues.
  • • Possible Factor: Kinematic latency or advisory delay.
  • • Insufficient Evidence: Explicit notice when logs are corrupted.
Contributing Factors Summary

Aggregates primary and secondary contributing factors into an explainable matrix (driver action, vehicle subsystem integrity, roadway infrastructure, and environment) — providing defensible decision support without premature legal blame.

Evidence-Based Forensic Standard: All directives cite specific EDR sensor channels, CAN message IDs, and timestamp correlations.
